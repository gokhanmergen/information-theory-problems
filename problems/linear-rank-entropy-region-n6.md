## Statement

For $n \ge 1$, a vector $h : 2^{[n]} \to \mathbb{R}$ is a **linear rank vector** (over $\mathbb{Q}$, or more generally over a field $\mathbb{F}$) if there exist a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, subspaces $V_1, \dots, V_n \le V$, and a positive scaling constant $c > 0$ such that
$$h(S) = c \cdot \dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left(\sum_{i \in S} V_i\right) \qquad \text{for all } S \subseteq [n].$$
Let $\mathcal{L}_n$ denote the convex cone generated by all linear rank vectors on $n$ variables (conic combinations of subspace rank vectors), and let $\overline{\mathcal{L}_n} \subset \mathbb{R}^{2^n}$ denote its topological closure.

**Problem (Polyhedrality of $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$; Dougherty–Freiling–Zeger, 2009).** Is the closed $6$-variable linear rank cone $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$ polyhedral — i.e., does there exist a *finite* list of linear rank inequalities $\sum_{S \subseteq [6]} a_{k,S} h(S) \ge 0$ ($k = 1, \dots, m$) that completely characterizes $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$?

(Equivalently, determine a complete finite list of linear rank inequalities characterizing $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$, or prove that $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$ is non-polyhedral and requires infinitely many independent inequalities.)

## Background

Subspace rank functions $S \mapsto \dim(\sum_{i \in S} V_i)$ form the linear-algebraic core of the entropy cone $\overline{\Gamma^*_n}$: every linear rank vector over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ is the entropy vector of jointly uniform linear random variables (up to $\log_2 q$), and linear rank inequalities bound the achievable rate region of **linear network coding** and **linear secret sharing** schemes.

For $n \le 3$, all polymatroids are linearly representable ($\overline{\mathcal{L}_n} = \Gamma_n$). For $n = 4$, Hammer, Romashchenko, Shen, and Vereshchagin (2000) proved that Shannon's polymatroid inequalities together with **Ingleton's inequality** (1971) completely characterize $\overline{\mathcal{L}_4}$, which is therefore polyhedral — in sharp contrast to Matúš's (2007) theorem that the 4-variable almost-entropic cone $\overline{\Gamma^*_4}$ is **non-polyhedral**. In a landmark 2009 paper, Dougherty, Freiling, and Zeger proved that for $n = 5$, adding exactly 24 new explicit linear rank inequalities to the Shannon and Ingleton inequalities completely characterizes $\overline{\mathcal{L}_5}$, so $\overline{\mathcal{L}_5}$ is still polyhedral. At $n = 6$, however, the combinatorial complexity explodes, and whether $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$ remains polyhedral or becomes non-polyhedral is a major open problem in information theory and matroid theory.

## What is known

- **$n \le 3$:** $\overline{\mathcal{L}_n} = \Gamma_n$ (polyhedral, characterized by Shannon inequalities alone).
- **$n = 4$ (Ingleton, 1971; Hammer–Romashchenko–Shen–Vereshchagin, 2000):** $\overline{\mathcal{L}_4}$ is polyhedral, completely characterized by Shannon inequalities plus the 6 permutations of Ingleton's inequality.
- **$n = 5$ (Dougherty–Freiling–Zeger, 2009):** $\overline{\mathcal{L}_5}$ is polyhedral, completely characterized by Shannon inequalities, Ingleton inequalities, and 24 explicit new linear rank inequalities (up to symmetry, all valid over every field).
- **$n = 6$ (Dougherty–Freiling–Zeger, 2009; Dougherty, 2014):** Over 1,000 new independent linear rank inequalities on 6 variables have been discovered computationally (including inequalities derived from common-information / Ahlswede–Körner–style subspace extensions and non-Pappus / Desarguesian matroid obstructions). Some inequalities for $n \ge 6$ even depend on the **characteristic** of the underlying field (e.g., Fano vs. non-Fano matroids at $n = 7$ and characteristic-dependent rank inequalities).
- **Polyhedrality for $n = 6$:** It remains completely open whether $\overline{\mathcal{L}_6}$ (over $\mathbb{Q}$, or over all fields) is polyhedral or infinitely faceted.
