\begin{abstract}
Empirical scaling laws in deep learning demonstrate that held-out cross-entropy loss decreases predictably as a power law of model parameters $N$, dataset tokens $D$, and compute budget $C \approx 6ND$, following the parametric form $L(N,D) = E + A N^{-\alpha_N} + B D^{-\alpha_D}$. Over the past several years, theoretical research across Information Theory, Differential Geometry, Statistical Physics, and Approximation Theory has revealed the exact mathematical mechanisms that give rise to these power laws. 

This monograph provides a self-contained, rigorous, and pedagogically accessible synthesis of these theoretical foundations. We systematically review: \textbf{(i) Information Theory and Rate--Distortion Theory}, clarifying why neural scaling reflects lossy compression of infinite-dimensional heavy-tailed sources ($D(R) \sim R^{-\rho}$) rather than classical channel coding error exponents; \textbf{(ii) Language Statistics and Horizon-Limited Scaling}, examining Cagnetta et al.'s derivation of the data exponent $\alpha_D = \gamma/(2\beta)$ from token correlations and conditional entropy decay; \textbf{(iii) High-Dimensional Geometry and Approximation Bounds}, detailing Sharma--Kaplan manifold tiling ($N^{-4/d}$), Bahri et al.'s four-regime phase diagram, and DeVore--Howard--Micchelli $n$-width lower bounds; \textbf{(iv) Statistical Physics and Kernel Dynamics}, presenting exact dual scaling laws for linear models $\Theta(N^{-(b-1)} + D^{-(b-1)/a})$ and Neural Tangent Kernel spectra; and \textbf{(v) Combinatorial Quanta and Superposition}, analyzing Zipf-distributed skill quantization and interference noise scaling. 

To bridge diverse disciplines, we provide an intuitive ``Rosetta Stone'' translation across mathematical dialects, step-by-step proofs of foundational theorems, and an explicit analysis of the ``honest gap'' between theoretical parameterizations and empirical observation.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The Empirical Phenomenon and Empirical Fits}

Let $N$ denote model capacity (typically the non-embedding parameter count), $D$ the dataset size (the number of processed training tokens), and $C \approx 6ND$ the floating-point operations (FLOPs) expended during autoregressive transformer pretraining. A ubiquitous empirical finding in deep learning is that validation loss $L(N, D)$ decays smoothly over multiple orders of magnitude according to a joint power law:
\begin{equation}
  L(N,D) = E + \left(\frac{N_c}{N}\right)^{\alpha_N} + \left(\frac{D_c}{D}\right)^{\alpha_D} = E + A N^{-\alpha_N} + B D^{-\alpha_D},
  \label{eq:additive-fit}
\end{equation}
where $E$ represents the irreducible entropy floor (the Bayes risk of the underlying data distribution), $N_c, D_c, A, B > 0$ are fit constants, and $\alpha_N, \alpha_D > 0$ are characteristic scaling exponents.

Equation~\eqref{eq:additive-fit} was documented across multiple modalities by Hestness et al.~\cite{hestness2017deep} and Rosenfeld et al.~\cite{rosenfeld2020constructive}, and popularized for large language models by Kaplan et al.~\cite{kaplan2020scaling} and Hoffmann et al. (Chinchilla)~\cite{hoffmann2022training}. In standard English autoregressive language modeling, typical empirical values fall in the ranges:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_N \approx 0.08 \text{ to } 0.34, \quad \alpha_D \approx 0.10 \text{ to } 0.28, \quad E \approx 1.5 \text{ to } 2.1 \text{ nats/token}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Hierarchy of Scientific Evidence}

Throughout this monograph, we classify theoretical statements according to four explicit levels of epistemic certainty:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.0em]
  \item \textbf{Rigorous Mathematical Theorem:} A closed-form theorem with complete proof for a fully specified mathematical source, function space, loss metric, and algorithm (e.g., Gaussian rate--distortion reverse water-filling, DeVore--Howard--Micchelli $n$-width lower bounds, Yang--Barron minimax rates).
  \item \textbf{Controlled Solvable Model:} An exact analytic or asymptotic result derived in a stylized model (e.g., random feature models, high-dimensional ridge regression, infinite-width Neural Tangent Kernel limits, associative memory networks).
  \item \textbf{Mechanistic Proposal:} A proposed physical or geometric mechanism transferring insights from a solvable model to finite, trained neural networks, accompanied by testable, falsifiable assumptions (e.g., Voronoi manifold tiling, Zipfian quantization of skills, token-correlation horizon cutoff).
  \item \textbf{Empirical Observation:} Parametric curves fitted to observed training runs over a finite resource window.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What Empirical Scaling Laws Establish in Practice}
\label{sec:empirical_laws}

\subsection{The Chinchilla Compute-Optimal Allocation Rule}

Given a total training compute budget $C \approx 6ND$ FLOPs, how should compute be optimally partitioned between parameter count $N$ and token count $D$? Minimizing the empirical loss ansatz $L(N, D) = E + A N^{-\alpha_N} + B D^{-\alpha_D}$ subject to $6ND = C$ yields the Lagrange stationarity condition:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_N A N^{-\alpha_N} = \alpha_D B D^{-\alpha_D}.
\end{equation}
Solving for $N_{\mathrm{opt}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{opt}}$ as functions of $C$ produces the optimal allocation frontiers:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{N_{\mathrm{opt}}(C) \propto C^{\frac{\alpha_D}{\alpha_N + \alpha_D}}, \qquad D_{\mathrm{opt}}(C) \propto C^{\frac{\alpha_N}{\alpha_N + \alpha_D}}}.
  \label{eq:chinchilla-allocation}
\end{equation}

For the empirical parameters reported by Hoffmann et al.~\cite{hoffmann2022training} ($\alpha_N = 0.34, \alpha_D = 0.28$), the optimal scaling exponents are:
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{opt}} \propto C^{\frac{0.28}{0.34 + 0.28}} = C^{0.45}, \qquad D_{\mathrm{opt}} \propto C^{\frac{0.34}{0.34 + 0.28}} = C^{0.55}.
\end{equation}
This establishes the **Chinchilla Prescription**: model size and training data should scale in roughly equal proportions ($N \propto D$), in stark contrast to earlier Kaplan et al.~\cite{kaplan2020scaling} estimates that favored scaling parameters much faster than tokens ($N \propto C^{0.73}, D \propto C^{0.27}$).

Recent replication and sensitivity studies have clarified this discrepancy. Besiroglu et al.~\cite{besiroglu2024chinchilla} demonstrated statistical sensitivity in the nonlinear regression fits. Porian et al.~\cite{porian2024resolving} showed that accounting for non-embedding parameter FLOPs, learning rate warmup schedules, and scale-dependent optimizer tuning fully reconciles the Kaplan and Chinchilla datasets.

\subsection{Essential Distinctions: Loss vs. Capabilities and Broken Scaling}

Two critical distinctions must be maintained when interpreting scaling laws:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Continuous Cross-Entropy Loss vs. Discontinuous Capability Metrics:} 
  While pretraining cross-entropy loss $L(N, D)$ improves smoothly as a power law, downstream accuracy on discrete tasks (e.g., arithmetic, multi-step code execution, translation benchmarks) often appears to jump discontinuously at critical thresholds. Schaeffer, Miranda, and Koyejo~\cite{schaeffer2023mirage} proved that these ``emergent abilities''~\cite{wei2022emergent} are frequently mathematical artifacts of nonlinear, thresholded metrics (such as exact-string match $0/1$ accuracy). When evaluated on smooth, continuous metrics (such as token edit distance or average log-probability), the underlying capabilities improve smoothly and predictably with scale.
  \item \textbf{Single Power Laws vs. Broken Power Laws:}
  Empirical scaling laws are valid over finite measurement windows. Caballero et al.~\cite{caballero2023broken} introduced broken power-law fits, formalizing how curves transition between different scaling regimes, experience plateauing due to data repetition~\cite{muennighoff2023scaling}, or saturate as they approach the irreducible entropy floor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross-Entropy Decomposition and the Irreducible Bayes Floor}

Let $\mathcal{X}^\mathbb{N}$ be the space of infinite token sequences drawn from an underlying stationary, ergodic ground-truth language process $P^*$. Let $Z_t = (X_1, \dots, X_t)$ denote the conditioning context up to step $t$, and $Q(X_{t+1} \mid Z_t)$ denote the model's predictive distribution. The expected cross-entropy loss decomposes exactly into:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}_{P^*}[-\log Q(X_{t+1}\mid Z_t)] = H_{P^*}(X_{t+1}\mid Z_t) + \mathbb{E}_{Z_t}\left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(P^*(\cdot\mid Z_t)\,\|\,Q(\cdot\mid Z_t)\right) \right].
  \label{eq:cross-entropy}
\end{equation}
Because the Kullback--Leibler divergence is non-negative and vanishes if and only if $Q \equiv P^*$, the conditional source entropy $H_{P^*}(X_{t+1}\mid Z_t)$ represents the **absolute, unbeatable Bayes risk** for log loss.

For any stationary process on a finite vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$, the conditional entropy $H(X_{t+1}\mid X_1^t)$ is monotonically non-increasing in $t$ and converges to the source entropy rate~\cite{shannon1948mathematical,coverthomas2006elements}:
\begin{equation}
  h = \lim_{t \to \infty} H(X_{t+1}\mid X_1^t) = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t} H(X_1, \dots, X_t).
\end{equation}
Thus, the floor $E$ in empirical scaling laws satisfies $E \ge h$. Claude Shannon's 1951 human letter-guessing experiment~\cite{shannon1951prediction} estimated the entropy of English text to be approximately $0.6 \text{ to } 1.3 \text{ bits/character}$ ($\approx 0.4 \text{ to } 0.9 \text{ nats/char}$). Frontier autoregressive models achieve validation cross-entropy losses near $1.0\text{--}1.4\text{ nats/token}$ ($\approx 0.6\text{--}0.85\text{ bits/char}$), approaching this fundamental Shannon limit.

\subsection{Why Scaling is Rate--Distortion, Not a Channel Reliability Exponent}
\label{subsec:channel_vs_rd}

A common heuristic intuition attempts to link neural scaling to Shannon's **channel coding reliability error exponent**. We formalize why this analogy is structurally flawed and establish that the correct Shannon-theoretic framework is **Rate--Distortion Theory**.

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Conceptual Intuition: Error Exponents vs. Distortion--Rate}]
\textbf{Channel Coding Error Exponent:} For communication over a noisy channel with blocklength $n$ at transmission rate $R < C$, the probability of decoding error decays \emph{exponentially}:
$$P_e(n) \le e^{-n E_r(R)}.$$
\textbf{Neural Scaling Law:} The excess test loss decays \emph{polynomially} in parameter count $N$:
$$L(N) - E \sim N^{-\alpha_N}.$$
Why the difference? In channel coding, independent channel noise across $n$ channel uses drives exponential concentration of typical sets. In neural scaling, parameters $N$ provide a compressed representation ($R \approx Nb$ bits) of an infinite-dimensional, continuous or combinatorial data distribution. Excess loss measures the reconstruction distortion $D(R)$, which obeys a power law whenever the source covariance spectrum has a heavy polynomial tail.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian Reverse Water-Filling on Power-Law Spectra]
\label{thm:rd}
Let $X$ be a zero-mean Gaussian random element in a separable Hilbert space with covariance eigenvalues decaying as a power law:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_k = c \cdot k^{-(1+\rho)}, \qquad c > 0, \quad \rho > 0, \quad k \in \mathbb{N}^+.
\end{equation}
Under mean squared error distortion $D = \mathbb{E}[\|X - \hat{X}\|^2]$, the distortion--rate function $D(R)$ in the asymptotic high-rate regime ($R \to \infty$) satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{D(R) \asymp R^{-\rho}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Gaussian rate--distortion theory~\cite{berger1971rate,coverthomas2006elements}, the optimal rate $R(\theta)$ and distortion $D(\theta)$ are parameterized by the water-filling threshold $\theta > 0$:
\begin{equation}
  D(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \min(\theta, \lambda_k), \qquad R(\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^\infty \log_2 \max\left(1, \frac{\lambda_k}{\theta}\right).
\end{equation}
Only modes with $\lambda_k > \theta$ are actively encoded. The number of active modes is:
\begin{equation}
  K(\theta) = \max\{ k \in \mathbb{N} : c k^{-(1+\rho)} \ge \theta \} = \left\lfloor \left(\frac{c}{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}} \right\rfloor \asymp \theta^{-\frac{1}{1+\rho}}.
\end{equation}
For active modes $k \le K(\theta)$, we have $\frac{\lambda_k}{\theta} = \left(\frac{K}{k}\right)^{1+\rho}$. The total rate in bits is:
\begin{align}
  R(\theta) &= \frac{1+\rho}{2} \sum_{k=1}^{K(\theta)} \log_2\left(\frac{K}{k}\right) = \frac{1+\rho}{2} \log_2\left(\frac{K^K}{K!}\right) \nonumber\\
  &= \frac{1+\rho}{2 \ln 2} \left[ K \ln K - (K \ln K - K + \mathcal{O}(\ln K)) \right] \nonumber\\
  &= \frac{(1+\rho)\log_2 e}{2} K(\theta) + \mathcal{O}(\log K) \asymp K(\theta).
\end{align}
Thus, the description rate is directly proportional to the number of active modes: $R \asymp K \asymp \theta^{-\frac{1}{1+\rho}}$, which implies $\theta(R) \asymp R^{-(1+\rho)}$.

The total distortion $D(\theta)$ is the sum of noise injected into active modes plus the unencoded tail variance:
\begin{align}
  D(\theta) &= K(\theta)\theta + \sum_{k = K(\theta)+1}^\infty c k^{-(1+\rho)} \nonumber\\
  &\approx K(\theta)\theta + \int_{K(\theta)}^\infty c x^{-(1+\rho)} dx = K(\theta)\theta + \frac{c}{\rho} K(\theta)^{-\rho}.
\end{align}
Substituting $K(\theta) \asymp (c/\theta)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}$ into $D(\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
  D(\theta) \asymp \theta \left(\frac{c}{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}} + \frac{c}{\rho} \left[\left(\frac{c}{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}}\right]^{-\rho} = \left(1 + \frac{1}{\rho}\right) c^{\frac{1}{1+\rho}} \theta^{\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}}.
\end{equation}
Finally, substituting $\theta(R) \asymp R^{-(1+\rho)}$ gives:
\begin{equation}
  D(R) \asymp \left( R^{-(1+\rho)} \right)^{\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}} = R^{-\rho}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-Dimensional vs. Heavy-Tailed Spectra]
If the source is strictly $d$-dimensional ($\lambda_k = 0$ for $k > d$), all $d$ modes are active at high rate, yielding exponential distortion decay:
\begin{equation}
  D(R) \asymp 2^{-2R/d} = \exp\left( -\frac{2 \ln 2}{d} R \right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Foundational Takeaway: The Spectral Fingerprint}]
Power-law scaling $L(N) \sim N^{-\alpha}$ is the **mathematical fingerprint of an effectively infinite-dimensional, heavy-tailed data spectrum** ($\lambda_k \sim k^{-(1+\rho)}$). If real data were finite-dimensional, optimal compression would yield exponential error decay.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Metric Entropy and Yang--Barron Minimax Estimation}

Yang and Barron (1999)~\cite{yang1999information} established the definitive information-theoretic bounds for statistical estimation by linking minimax risk to the Kolmogorov $\varepsilon$-covering entropy $H(\varepsilon; \mathcal{P}) = \log \mathcal{N}(\varepsilon, \mathcal{P}, \rho)$.

\begin{theorem}[Yang--Barron Minimax Balance~\cite{yang1999information,tsybakov2009introduction}]
Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a family of probability distributions on $\mathcal{X}$. If the metric entropy under Hellinger distance satisfies $H(\varepsilon; \mathcal{P}) \asymp \varepsilon^{-r}$ for some $r > 0$, then the minimax estimation risk over $n$ i.i.d. samples satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \inf_{\hat{p}_n} \sup_{p^* \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_{p^*} \left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^* \| \hat{p}_n) \right] \asymp \varepsilon_n^2,
\end{equation}
where the minimax optimal rate $\varepsilon_n$ is the unique solution to the balance equation:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{H(\varepsilon_n; \mathcal{P}) \asymp n \varepsilon_n^2}.
  \label{eq:entropy-balance}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Sobolev Nonparametric Rates]
For target densities in a Sobolev ball $W^s(\mathbb{R}^d)$ of smoothness $s$ in $d$ dimensions, classical metric entropy gives $H(\varepsilon) \asymp \varepsilon^{-d/s}$. Solving $\varepsilon_n^{-d/s} \asymp n \varepsilon_n^2$ gives $\varepsilon_n^{2 + d/s} \asymp n^{-1}$, yielding the minimax risk rate:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_n \asymp n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}} \implies \alpha_D = \frac{2s}{2s+d}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Model/Data Allocation: Jeon \& Van Roy (2024)}

Jeon and Van Roy~\cite{jeon2024information} formalized neural scaling through a Bayesian rate--distortion decomposition. For a dataset $\mathcal{D}_T$ of $T$ observations and a target data-generating process $F$ approximated by a compressed surrogate model $\widetilde{F}$, the expected excess log loss decomposes as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_T \ \lesssim \ \underbrace{\frac{I(F;\widetilde{F})}{T}}_{\text{Estimation / Information Cost}} \ + \ \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_F(\cdot\mid X)\,\|\,P_{\widetilde{F}}(\cdot\mid X))}_{\text{Representation Misspecification Cost}}.
  \label{eq:jeon}
\end{equation}
The first term is the statistical price of identifying $\widetilde{F}$ from $T$ tokens, which decreases as $T$ increases. The second term is the structural approximation error, which decreases as surrogate capacity $N$ increases. For an infinite-width two-layer network prior, optimizing this bound under a compute constraint $C \approx NT$ rigorously yields an optimal data-to-model allocation $N \propto T$ up to logarithmic factors, providing an information-theoretic foundation for the Chinchilla scaling rule.

\subsection{Hilberg's Law and Long-Range Context Correlations}

In 1990, Wolfgang Hilberg~\cite{hilberg1990der} conjectured that natural language possesses long-range power-law memory. Specifically, the block entropy of $n$ consecutive words or characters scales as:
\begin{equation}
  H(X_1^n) = h n + c n^\delta + o(n^\delta), \qquad 0 < \delta < 1 \quad (\text{typically } \delta \approx 0.5).
  \label{eq:hilberg}
\end{equation}
Differentiating or differencing Equation~\eqref{eq:hilberg} implies that the excess conditional entropy of the $(n+1)$-th token given context length $n$ decays as a power law~\cite{debowski2015relaxed}:
\begin{equation}
  H(X_{n+1}\mid X_1^n) - h \asymp n^{-\gamma}, \qquad \text{where } \gamma = 1 - \delta.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivation of the Data Scaling Exponent: Cagnetta et al. (2024/2026)}
\label{subsec:cagnetta_derivation}

A milestone breakthrough by Cagnetta, Ravent\'os, Ganguli, and Wyart~\cite{cagnetta2026deriving} established an analytical link between measurable language statistics and the empirical data scaling exponent $\alpha_D$.

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Conceptual Intuition: Reading with Noisy Glasses}]
Imagine learning to predict the next word in a language by reading $D$ total tokens. 
1. Long-range grammatical and semantic correlations decay with distance as $|C(n)| \sim n^{-\beta}$.
2. Estimating these correlations from a finite dataset of $D$ tokens is corrupted by central limit sampling noise $\sigma_{\mathrm{sample}} \sim \mathcal{O}(D^{-1/2})$.
3. The model can only exploit correlations that rise above the noise floor: $n^{-\beta} \gtrsim D^{-1/2}$. This sets a maximum **statistically resolvable context horizon**:
   $$n^*(D) \sim D^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}.$$
4. If the model is expressive enough to master all patterns within this horizon, the remaining unpredicted entropy is governed by the conditional entropy gap at horizon $n^*$:
   $$\mathcal{L}(D) - H_\infty \sim (n^*(D))^{-\gamma} = \left(D^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}\right)^{-\gamma} = D^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\beta}}.$$
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{theorem}[Cagnetta--Ravent\'os--Ganguli--Wyart Scaling Law~\cite{cagnetta2026deriving}]
Suppose a language corpus satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Conditional Entropy Decay:} $H_n - H_\infty \asymp n^{-\gamma}$, where $H_n = H(X_{t+1}\mid X_{t-n+1}^t)$.
  \item \textbf{Token Covariance Decay:} $\|\mathbf{\Sigma}(n)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \asymp n^{-\beta}$, where $\mathbf{\Sigma}(n)$ is the token correlation matrix at lag $n$.
  \item \textbf{Fast Within-Horizon Learning:} Within the resolvable horizon $n \le n^*(D)$, the model learns patterns sufficiently fast that the dominant bottleneck is context truncation at $n^*(D)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, when trained on a dataset of $D$ tokens in the data-limited regime, the excess cross-entropy loss obeys:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\mathcal{L}(D) - H_\infty \asymp D^{-\alpha_D}, \qquad \text{where } \alpha_D = \frac{\gamma}{2\beta}}.
  \label{eq:cagnetta}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Empirical Validation without Parameter Fitting.}
Cagnetta et al. measured $\gamma$ and $\beta$ directly on language corpora and compared the predicted $\alpha_D = \gamma/(2\beta)$ against actual training curves of GPT-2 and LLaMA transformers:
\begin{align}
  \text{\textbf{TinyStories:}}&\quad \gamma = 0.325 \pm 0.003, \quad \beta = 0.88 \pm 0.06 \nonumber\\
  &\quad \implies \alpha_D^{\mathrm{pred}} = 0.185 \pm 0.013 \quad (\alpha_D^{\mathrm{emp}} \approx 0.18), \\
  \text{\textbf{WikiText-103:}}&\quad \gamma = 0.265 \pm 0.016, \quad \beta = 0.94 \pm 0.16 \nonumber\\
  &\quad \implies \alpha_D^{\mathrm{pred}} = 0.141 \pm 0.025 \quad (\alpha_D^{\mathrm{emp}} \approx 0.14).
\end{align}
The empirical scaling curves collapse onto a universal curve under the theoretical rescaling $D \mapsto D / n^{2\beta}$ and $\mathcal{L}_n \mapsto n^\gamma \mathcal{L}_n$. This represents the first parameter-free, first-principles prediction of a neural scaling exponent from measurable corpus statistics.

\subsection{Controlled Data Structure: Random Walk Graphs (Barkeshli et al., 2026)}

Barkeshli, Alfarano, and Gromov~\cite{barkeshli2026origin} trained transformers on synthetic language generated by random walks on graphs with tunable topology. They found that even for Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi graphs lacking heavy-tailed power-law spectra, neural networks exhibit power-law scaling over finite ranges. This demonstrates that while heavy-tailed language statistics analytically set exponents in the horizon-limited regime, neural optimization dynamics can also generate effective power-law scaling over finite windows.

\subsection{Manifold Tiling Geometry: Sharma \& Kaplan (2020)}
\label{subsec:sharma_kaplan}

High-dimensional data in $\mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{amb}}}$ typically concentrates on a lower-dimensional Riemannian submanifold $\mathcal{M}$ of intrinsic dimension $d \ll D_{\mathrm{amb}}$. Sharma and Kaplan~\cite{sharma2020neural} derived the parameter exponent $\alpha_N$ by modeling neural networks as piecewise-affine approximators on $\mathcal{M}$.

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Geometric Intuition: Tiling a Curved Manifold with Flat Tiles}]
Consider approximating a curved 2D surface ($d=2$) in 3D space using $N$ flat triangular tiles:
1. Each tile has a characteristic linear diameter $\varepsilon \asymp N^{-1/d} = N^{-1/2}$.
2. A smooth $\mathcal{C}^2$ target function $f^*$ curves away from each flat affine tile $\hat{f}$ by its second-order Taylor remainder:
   $$|f^*(x) - \hat{f}(x)| \le \frac{1}{2} \|x - x_0\|^2 \|\nabla^2 f^*(x_0)\| \sim \varepsilon^2 = N^{-2/d}.$$
3. Integrating the squared error over all $N$ tiles yields the mean squared loss:
   $$L(N) = \int_{\mathcal{M}} |f^*(x) - \hat{f}(x)|^2 d\mu(x) \asymp \varepsilon^4 = (N^{-1/d})^4 = N^{-4/d}.$$
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{theorem}[Manifold Tiling Exponent~\cite{sharma2020neural}]
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a compact $d$-dimensional Riemannian manifold and $f^* \in \mathcal{C}^2(\mathcal{M})$. If a neural network with $N$ parameters partitions $\mathcal{M}$ into $K \asymp N$ local linear regions of size $\varepsilon \asymp N^{-1/d}$, then under squared error regression:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{L(N) - E \asymp N^{-4/d} \implies \alpha_N = \frac{4}{d}}.
\end{equation}
If the target function is only Lipschitz continuous ($\mathcal{C}^1$), the first-order error yields $\alpha_N = 2/d$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Intrinsic Dimension Measurements.}
For GPT models where empirical parameter scaling gives $\alpha_N \approx 0.076$, the geometric formula predicts an effective intrinsic dimension $d \approx 4 / \alpha_N \approx 53$. Sharma and Kaplan measured intrinsic dimensions of transformer token activations and found $d \approx 7$ within a single contiguous 1024-token document, but $d > 90$ across heterogeneous documents, revealing a scale-dependent multi-scale manifold hierarchy.

\subsection{Fundamental Impossibility Lower Bounds in Approximation Theory}
\label{subsec:impossibility_bounds}

Theoretical scaling laws are bounded by foundational impossibility theorems in approximation theory, information theory, and computational complexity:

\begin{theorem}[DeVore--Howard--Micchelli Continuous Non-Linear $n$-Width Lower Bound (1989)~\cite{devore1989optimal}]
Let $W^s_p([0, 1]^d)$ be the Sobolev ball of smoothness $s$ in $d$ dimensions. Let $\mathcal{M}_N = \{g(\theta, \cdot) : \theta \in \mathbb{R}^N\}$ be any parametric family where parameters $\theta(f)$ are selected continuously from $f$. Then the continuous non-linear $n$-width satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{d_N(W^s_p([0, 1]^d))_q \ge c \cdot N^{-s/d}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
This theorem proves that **no continuous parameter optimization method** (including gradient descent on neural networks) can ever circumvent the curse of dimensionality $N^{-s/d}$ for worst-case Sobolev functions.

\begin{theorem}[Rissanen MDL Parametric Redundancy Bound (1986, 1996)~\cite{rissanen1986stochastic,rissanen1996fisher}]
For any regular, identifiable $k$-dimensional parametric family $\{p_\theta : \theta \in \Theta \subset \mathbb{R}^k\}$, universal coding incurs an unavoidable minimax parameter redundancy:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\inf_q \sup_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta} \left[ \log \frac{p_\theta(X^n)}{q(X^n)} \right] \ge \frac{k}{2} \log n + \frac{k}{2} \log\left(\frac{1}{2\pi e}\right) + \log \int_\Theta \sqrt{\det I(\theta)} \, d\theta + o(1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Statistical Query (SQ) Lower Bounds (Kearns 1998, Shamir 2020)~\cite{kearns1998efficient}]
For parity functions on $d$ binary bits, any algorithm with Statistical Query access with tolerance $\tau \ge 2^{-d/3}$ requires at least $2^{\Omega(d)}$ queries. Furthermore, under cryptographic assumptions, there exist functions that no polynomial-size neural network can learn with loss advantage $> 2^{-\Omega(d)}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Takeaway: Scaling Laws are Data-Contingent}]
Neural scaling laws $L \sim N^{-\alpha}$ are **not universal mathematical laws of all conceivable functions**. They reflect the structural properties of natural multimodal data: low intrinsic manifold dimensionality, heavy-tailed spectral decay, and compositional hierarchy.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Kernel Spectra and the Hard-Cutoff Heuristic}
\label{subsec:kernel_spectra}

In the infinite-width Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime~\cite{jacot2018neural,chizat2019lazy}, neural network learning curves can be solved analytically. Let $T_K$ be the kernel integral operator with eigenvalues $\lambda_k \asymp k^{-a}$ ($a > 1$) and target function projections $c_k^2 \asymp k^{-b}$ ($b > 1$).

Bordelon, Canatar, and Pehlevan~\cite{bordelon2020spectrum,canatar2021spectral} established that neural networks exhibit **spectral bias**: high-eigenvalue (low-frequency) modes are learned first. Suppose a learning algorithm resolves all modes up to an effective spectral cutoff $k_*$ and leaves higher modes unlearned. The residual error is the unlearned target power:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E} \approx \sum_{k > k_*} c_k^2 \asymp \int_{k_*}^\infty x^{-b} dx \asymp k_*^{-(b-1)}.
  \label{eq:hard-cutoff}
\end{equation}

For **ridgeless kernel regression** on $n$ samples, the effective regularization parameter $\kappa$ solves $n \asymp \sum_k \frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_k + \kappa} \asymp \kappa^{-1/a} \implies \kappa \asymp n^{-a}$. The cutoff mode satisfies $\lambda_{k_*} \asymp \kappa \implies k_*^{-a} \asymp n^{-a} \implies k_* \asymp n$.
Substituting $k_* \asymp n$ into Equation~\eqref{eq:hard-cutoff} gives:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}(n) \asymp n^{-(b-1)}.
\end{equation}
Under noisy labels with optimal regularization, Cui et al.~\cite{cui2021generalization} show the exponent transitions to $n^{-(b-1)/b}$.

\subsection{Exact Joint $(N, D)$ Scaling Laws in Linear Regression (Lin et al., NeurIPS 2024)}
\label{subsec:lin_scaling}

Lin, Wu, Kakade, Bartlett, and Lee~\cite{lin2024scaling} (see also Lin, Dobriban, Hong~\cite{lin2024nonasymptotic}) derived exact non-asymptotic and asymptotic joint scaling laws for high-dimensional linear regression trained by one-pass SGD with a random feature sketch of size $N$ on $D$ samples.

\begin{theorem}[Lin et al. (NeurIPS 2024)~\cite{lin2024scaling}]
Let covariate covariance eigenvalues decay as $\lambda_k \asymp k^{-a}$ ($a > 1$) and target coefficients decay as $c_k^2 \asymp k^{-b}$ ($1 < b < a+1$). Under one-pass SGD with feature sketch dimension $N$ and sample count $D$, the excess generalization error satisfies:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\mathcal{E}(N, D) = \Theta\left( N^{-(b-1)} + D^{-\frac{b-1}{a}} \right)}.
  \label{eq:lin}
\end{equation}
Under an isotropic parameter prior ($b = a$), this simplifies to $\Theta(N^{-(a-1)} + D^{-\frac{a-1}{a}})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{Intuition: Why Do the Model and Data Exponents Differ?}]
Both terms in Equation~\eqref{eq:lin} originate from the cutoff formula $\mathcal{E} \asymp k_*^{-(b-1)}$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item **Parameter Cutoff:** Projecting into an $N$-dimensional sketch truncates modes at index $k_* = N$. The residual error is $k_*^{-(b-1)} = N^{-(b-1)}$.
  \item **One-Pass SGD Data Cutoff:** One-pass SGD does not invert the full covariance matrix. A mode $k$ is learned only when accumulated gradient signal over $D$ steps reaches order one: $D \lambda_k \gtrsim 1 \implies D k^{-a} \gtrsim 1 \implies k_* \asymp D^{1/a}$. The residual error is $k_*^{-(b-1)} = (D^{1/a})^{-(b-1)} = D^{-\frac{b-1}{a}}$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Key Prediction:} $\alpha_N = b - 1$ and $\alpha_D = (b-1)/a \implies \alpha_N = a \cdot \alpha_D$. For Chinchilla empirical values ($\alpha_N = 0.34, \alpha_D = 0.28$), this implies $a \approx 1.21$ and $b \approx 1.34$, perfectly within the theoretical admissible window.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Dynamical Training Phases: Maloney et al., Bordelon et al., Paquette et al.}

Maloney, Roberts, and Sully~\cite{maloney2022solvable} provided a solvable random-feature model demonstrating distinct compute-optimal phases. Bordelon, Atanasov, and Pehlevan~\cite{bordelon2024dynamical} extended this to gradient descent dynamics, explaining why learning curve exponents differ across early-time and late-time width limits. Paquette et al.~\cite{paquette2024phases} rigorously proved the existence of $4+3$ distinct compute-optimal scaling phases depending on the relative complexity of data spectrum, target smoothness, and batch size.

\subsection{Associative Memories with Heavy-Tailed Inputs (Cabannes et al., 2024)}

They derived exact sample and parameter scaling curves for key--value associative memories, rigorously bridging discrete memory storage with neural scaling laws.

\subsection{The Quantization Model of Neural Scaling (Michaud et al., NeurIPS 2023)}
\label{subsec:quanta}

Michaud, Liu, Girit, and Tegmark~\cite{michaud2023quantization} proposed that knowledge consists of a large discrete set of independent sub-problems, facts, or skills (``quanta'') $\mathcal{Q} = \{q_1, q_2, \dots\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Quantization Scaling Exponent~\cite{michaud2023quantization}]
Let each quantum $q_i$ have utility $U_i \asymp i^{-q}$ with $q > 1$ (Zipf's law for skills). Under greedy parameter allocation, a network with $N$ parameters masters the top $K \asymp N$ most useful quanta. The residual loss from unlearned quanta is:
\begin{equation}
  L(N) - E = \sum_{i = N+1}^\infty U_i \approx \int_N^\infty x^{-q} dx = \frac{1}{q-1} N^{-(q-1)} \implies \boxed{\alpha_N = q - 1}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Hutter's Countable-Input Memorization Model (2021)}
\label{subsec:hutter}

Marcus Hutter~\cite{hutter2021learning} analyzed an optimal memorizer on countably many discrete inputs. If input probabilities follow a power law $\theta_i \propto i^{-(1+\eta)}$ ($\eta > 0$), a training set of $n$ samples observes all inputs with frequency $\theta_i \gtrsim 1/n \implies i_* \asymp n^{\frac{1}{1+\eta}}$. The expected test error over unseen inputs scales as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[E_n] = \sum_{i > i_*} \theta_i \asymp i_*^{-\eta} = \left( n^{\frac{1}{1+\eta}} \right)^{-\eta} = \boxed{n^{-\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Toy Models of Superposition (Elhage et al., Anthropic 2022)}
\label{subsec:superposition}

How does a neural network with activation width $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ represent $M \gg d_{\mathrm{model}}$ semantic features? Elhage et al.~\cite{elhage2022toy} formalized **neural superposition**:
\begin{theorem}[Johnson--Lindenstrauss Superposition and Interference Noise]
Let $M$ sparse, independent features have active probability $p \ll 1$ and unit variance. An overcomplete dictionary $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}} \times M}$ with unit-norm columns satisfies $|\langle w_i, w_j \rangle| \le \varepsilon$ in dimension $d_{\mathrm{model}} = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log M}{\varepsilon^2}\right)$ by the Johnson--Lindenstrauss lemma~\cite{johnson1984extensions}.

Linear reconstruction $\hat{x}_i = w_i^T (\mathbf{W} x) = x_i + \sum_{j \neq i} \langle w_i, w_j \rangle x_j$ suffers cross-talk interference variance:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\left[ \left(\sum_{j \neq i} \langle w_i, w_j \rangle x_j\right)^2 \right] = p(1-p) \sum_{j \neq i} |\langle w_i, w_j \rangle|^2 \approx \boxed{p(1-p) \frac{M}{d_{\mathrm{model}}}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
As hidden width $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ increases, interference noise decays as $\mathcal{O}(1/d_{\mathrm{model}})$, providing a representation capacity mechanism for power-law error reduction.

\section{The ``Honest Gap'' and Open Research Frontiers}
\label{sec:open_challenges}
%==============================================================================

Despite major theoretical breakthroughs, a fundamental epistemic gap remains between theory and practice:

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=\textbf{The Fundamental ``Honest Gap''}]
\textbf{No existing theory derives the empirical scaling exponents ($\alpha_N \approx 0.34, \alpha_D \approx 0.28$) for real natural language purely from first physical principles.}
Existing theories rigorously prove that scaling exponents are functions of underlying data statistics:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha = \Phi\left( a_{\mathrm{spectral}}, \, d_{\mathrm{manifold}}, \, q_{\mathrm{Zipf}}, \, \gamma_{\mathrm{entropy}}, \, \beta_{\mathrm{correlation}} \right).
\end{equation}
However, these parameters ($a, d, q, \gamma, \beta$) are **empirically measured rather than derived from axioms of human cognition or linguistics**. Deriving why human language generates these specific spectral exponents represents the ultimate frontier of deep learning theory.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Key Open Frontiers}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.0em]
  \item \textbf{Beating Power Laws via Data Pruning:}
  Sorscher, Geirhos, Shekhar, Ganguli, and Morcos~\cite{sorscher2022beyond} proved that optimizing data quality via metric-based pruning can break polynomial power laws, transitioning learning curves to **exponential scaling** ($\mathcal{E} \sim e^{-\alpha D}$). Developing an operational theory of ``effective data quality'' that incorporates deduplication and curriculum difficulty remains a high-priority challenge.
  \item \textbf{Test-Time Compute and Search Scaling:}
  Recent models (e.g., OpenAI o1/o3, Snell et al.~\cite{snell2024scaling}) demonstrate that spending compute at inference time (via tree search, chain-of-thought verification, and self-correction) yields scaling gains that can substitute for pretraining scale. A unified rate--distortion theory bridging pretraining parameter rate with test-time search budget is an active frontier.
  \item \textbf{Feature Learning Beyond Fixed Kernels:}
  Extending kernel and NTK scaling theories beyond the ``lazy training'' regime~\cite{chizat2019lazy} to capture representation learning, self-attention routing, and rotary positional embeddings.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%==============================================================================

Neural scaling laws are not empirical accidents; they are the rigorous macroscopic manifestation of scale-free mathematical structures.

By analyzing scaling through Shannon rate--distortion theory, we establish that power laws reflect lossy compression of heavy-tailed, infinite-dimensional data spectra under reverse water-filling. Through high-dimensional geometry and statistical physics, parameter and data exponents map directly to manifold dimensionality, kernel eigenspectra, and long-range correlation decay. While fundamental lower bounds (Yang--Barron, DeVore--Howard--Micchelli, Rissanen) establish unbeatable speed limits on learning, the rich hierarchical structure of natural language permits modern deep neural networks to operate along an optimal information-theoretic frontier.
